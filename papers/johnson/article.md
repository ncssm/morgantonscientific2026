---
title: A Comparative Analysis of the Effects of Astaxanthin and β-Sitostenone on a Danio rerio Model of Diabetic Retinopathy
short_title: "Astaxanthin and β-Sitostenone in a Danio rerio Model of Diabetic Retinopathy"
abstract:
    Diabetic retinopathy (DR) is the world’s leading cause of blindness in the working-age population. The current and most common treatments of DR include intravitreal injections and laser photocoagulation, both of which are invasive and associated with significant side-effects. As interest in non-invasive therapies increases, orally administered compounds with anti-oxidative and anti-angiogenic properties warrant investigation. This study comparatively evaluated the therapeutic potential of astaxanthin and β-sitostenone as oral treatments for DR using a zebrafish embryo (*Danio rerio*) model.
    
    DR was induced in zebrafish embryos via exposure to 130 mM dextrose. Embryos were treated with astaxanthin (10 and 100µg/L) or β-sitostenone (0.1 and 1 µM), and disease severity was assessed using heart rate analysis and an optokinetic response (OKR) assay to evaluate visual function. Elevated heart rate was observed in DR-induced larvae, consistent with systemic disease progression. While both astaxanthin and β-sitostenone significantly reduced DR-associated tachycardia, their effects on visual function diverged sharply. In the optokinetic response (OKR) assay, astaxanthin failed to produce a statistically significant recovery at any tested concentration. In contrast, β-sitostenone treatment significantly rescued saccadic frequency, with the 1 µM dose maintaining efficacy even at high rotational velocities.
    These findings suggest that while both compounds mitigate systemic symptoms of DR, β-sitostenone uniquely preserves visual acuity, marking it as a promising candidate for non-invasive DR therapy.

## Introduction
Worldwide, one in nine adults (20-79 years old) have diabetes. This statistic is projected to grow to 1 in 8 adults by the year 2050 (International Diabetes Federation, 2025). Diabetes can lead to several complications, with two general categories being microvascular and macrovascular. Microvascular diseases, including neuropathy, nephropathy, and retinopathy are more common complications of diabetes than macrovascular complications are [@papatheodorou_complications_2018]. Diabetic retinopathy (DR) is the leading cause of blindness in the working age population [@cheung_diabetic_2010]. It involves the blood vessels of the retina becoming damaged and is characterized by harmful neovascularization (angiogenesis), high reactive oxygen species (ROS), and oxidative stress. Over 25% of people with diabetes also suffer from DR as a complication, and its prevalence is still increasing [@lundeen_prevalence_2023].

The current and common treatments of DR include intravitreal injections of anti-VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factors) agents [@galluzzo_secukinumab_2022], and corticosteroids [@chawan-saad_corticosteroids_2019]. Although these treatments have demonstrated some benefit in treating diabetic retinopathy (DR), many patients continue to experience disease progression. This includes alterations in retinal thickness, disruption of the blood-retinal barrier, neurophysiological changes, development of macular edema, and the potential onset of neovascularization in more advanced stages [@rensing_endothelial_2012]. When drugs, like triamcinolone acetonide, are delivered to patients with DR through intravitreal injection, they are exposed to the risk of unintended drug diffusion into the systemic circulation [@tomita_updates_2021] and are at risk for severe ocular inflammation and damage through endophthalmitis [@busch_causative_2019]. These side effects highlight the urgency to develop oral alternatives, which can be delivered in low doses in comparison to traditional injections.

Nutraceuticals are naturally occurring functional foods that offer health and medical benefits. Some, like carotenoids, act as powerful antioxidants capable of neutralizing ROS. Others possess anti-inflammatory properties and have also been shown to support both neuronal and microvascular health. Because they are derived from natural foods, nutraceuticals are generally accessible, affordable, and considered safe for consumption [@arteaga_zebrafish_2021]. When used appropriately, they are less likely to cause side effects commonly linked to anti-diabetic drugs, such as hypoglycemia, liver or kidney damage, and gastrointestinal discomfort.

Astaxanthin, a xanthophyll carotenoid, can be extracted from a species of green algae (*Haematococcus pluvialis*) and has shown potential to be an effective anti-DR drug and has neuroprotective properties [@wu_astaxanthin_2015]. Astaxanthin has been observed to reduce reactive oxygen species (ROS), downregulate inflammation, and serve as an anti-VEGF for microvascular complications when administered orally [@ye_nutraceuticals_2024]. An increasing number of studies indicate that astaxanthin is effective in preventing and treating various eye diseases, affecting both the anterior and posterior segments of the eye [@giannaccare_clinical_2020]. Furthermore, astaxanthin is commonly found in the human diet in foods like red yeasts and aquatic species, including salmon, krill, trout, and fish eggs [@bharti_astaxanthin:_2025]. This demonstrates that astaxanthin is safer for the whole body at therapeutic doses than a more concentrated dose of a protein-inhibitor.

The active chemical β-sitostenone is an extract of *Euonymus alatus* (burning bush), a plant used in Chinese medicine for the treatment of diabetes [@fan_traditional_2020]. The chemical β-sitostenone has been shown to alleviate DR and oxygen induced retinopathy in mice and in vitro and minimize its side effects in comparison to traditional intravitreal injections like triamcinolone acetonide [@ji_stigmast-4-en-3-one_2024], but it has not yet been observed using a zebrafish model of DR. 

Zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) embryos have emerged as a valuable model organism for studying ocular development and disease due to their remarkable similarities to the human retinal structure and cell types, including comparable metabolic and phototransduction support systems. By 60 hours post-fertilization (hpf), zebrafish embryos develop a functional retina, making them particularly useful for early-stage vision research [@morris_studying_2005]. Additionally, zebrafish possess the unique ability to regenerate retinal tissue, offering insights into potential regenerative therapies. Their low maintenance cost and transparent embryos further enhance their utility, as they allow for real-time, direct visualization of eye structures and development [@veldman_zebrafish_2008].

In order to induce retinopathy, this study utilized 130 mM dextrose, the naturally occurring form of glucose (D-glucose) [@ji_stigmast-4-en-3-one_2024]. At this concentration, dextrose has been shown to expand hyaloid-retinal vessel diameter and induce angiogenesis while still maintaining low toxicity [@jung_high_2016].

This study aims to evaluate the therapeutic potential of two orally administered drugs in the treatment of a zebrafish embryo model of diabetic retinopathy. Astaxanthin and β-sitostenone have each been reported to attenuate angiogenesis in models of diabetic retinopathy; however, their effects have not been evaluated within the same model system nor subjected to direct comparative analysis. Given the increasing prevalence of diabetic retinopathy and the adverse effects associated with intravitreal injections, comparative evaluation of oral therapies is warranted. Furthermore, prioritizing naturally derived treatments is critical, as nutraceuticals demonstrate low toxicity profiles and offer broader accessibility due to their affordability and availability. To our knowledge, this is the first study to look at β-sitostenone and astaxanthin side by side for potential treatment of DR. It was hypothesized that if astaxanthin is administered to a zebrafish embryo model of diabetic retinopathy then it will prove to be a more efficient treatment for diabetic retinopathy than stigmast-4-en-3-one. Astaxanthin has been shown to suppress oxidative stress, downregulate inflammation, and help improve visual acuity and control blood dextrose in models of retinopathy [@ye_nutraceuticals_2024].
## Methods
### Zebrafish rearing
Wild-type (WT) zebrafish embryos were purchased from Carolina Biological. Embryos were collected using a plastic pipette and preserved for further analysis in 24-well plates with embryo medium. The embryos were dechorionated at 24 hpf in accordance with standard protocol [@henn_dechorionation_2011]. Immediately after dechorionation, embryos were placed into their respective control and experimental groups. Wells were divided into six groups of four wells with 4–8 embryos per group; 1–2 embryos per well. The two control groups were as follows: as a negative control with non-DR embryos and no treatment (labeled as E3), and a positive control with a 130mM dextrose solution to induce DR through hyperglycemia and no treatment (labeled as D+E3). The four experimental groups consisted of the following (@Figure_1):
1. LA+D+E3: 130 mM dextrose solution for DR + 10 μg/L astaxanthin [@dong_protective_2021]
2. HA+D+E3: 130 mM dextrose solution for DR + 100 μg/L astaxanthin [@dong_protective_2021]
3. LS+D+E3: 130 mM dextrose solution for DR + 0.1 μM β-sitostenone [@ji_stigmast-4-en-3-one_2024]
4. HS+D+E3: 130 mM dextrose solution for DR +1 μM β-sitostenone [@ji_stigmast-4-en-3-one_2024]\
To keep fresh and thus effective solutions, 130 mM dextrose was remade every 24 hours.
Since all experimental groups were made up in 130 mM dextrose, they were also remade
every 24 hours. Embryo media was remade at least every two weeks, using new embryo
media for each trial.

### Heart rate determination
Hyperglycemia has been found to elevate heart rates in zebrafish [@yanyi_activation_2017]. To ensure that DR had been induced in the embryos and to evaluate the therapeutic effects of astaxanthin and β-sitostenone, a heart rate analysis was performed. At 48 hpf, video imaging of each embryo’s heartbeat in lateral position was performed under a confocal microscope for 15 seconds at 30 frames per second. The number of heart beats that was counted was multiplied by four and recorded as beats per minute. If any larvae showed an absent or abnormal heart rate, they were  removed from their wells immediately to prevent potential contamination of other embryos and to attenuate confounding factors in the data. 

### Quantitative optokinetic response
Retinopathy disrupts blood vessels that provide photoreceptors with oxygen and nutrients, leading to hypoxia and oxidative stress, which leads to eventual photoreceptor cell dysfunction [@kern_photoreceptors_2015]. This assay served as a way to assess how damaged each group’s photoreceptor cells are and how strong their visual acuity is. After the larvae are 5 days post-fertilization (dpf), 1 larva per group was placed in a 35-mm petri dish filled with 6% methylcellulose near to the brim. Methylcellulose is necessary to immobilize the fish during visual imaging. The petri dish was placed onto a stereo microscope for imaging. By printing 3D parts and using an arduino microcontroller, the optokinetic response can be observed from all larvae. 

Controlled by the arduino, a 3D printed cylinder wall with 1 cm black and white alternating stripes will rotate at 6 rpm (rotations per minute) around the petri dish to assess the larvae’s visual acuity [@hermans_3d-printed_2024]. After this was observed, the 1 cm stripes were replaced with 0.5 cm stripes to determine which groups could pick up on the contrast. To analyze the spatial frequency of the stripes, the cycles per degree (CPD) was calculated for both widths of the stripes. The CPD was calculated using the following formula, where h represented the length of one cycle of the smallest grading at which OKR was observed, and a represented the distance from the center of the lens to the grading: $\frac{1}{2tan^{-1}(\frac{h}{2a})}$ 

Higher CPD values tend to be more difficult for zebrafish embryos to elicit an optokinetic response from, while smaller values, such as 0.12 CPD, have been observed to allow for higher optokinetic responses [@hollbach_photopic_2015].

Once the larva was secured in the petri dish and situated under the microscope, the recording was started from the camera, and the motor program through the Arduino controller was also started. This caused the black and white stripes to rotate around the immobilized larva. One at a time, each of the larvae were  assessed at three different speeds, each speed faster than the previous one, for 1 minute per speed. Each 1 minute video was split up into 10 second intervals, and the number of full saccades in each interval was counted and multiplied by 6 to be recorded as saccades per minute. This is a cost-efficient and easily set up method to perform in comparison to purchasing a premade apparatus. Statistical analysis, using a one-way ANOVA and a 95% confidence interval, was performed using GraphPad Prism software to obtain p-values and construct graphs.

### Zebrafish embryo euthanization
After 7 dpf, the zebrafish larvae were placed in a bleach solution (sodium hypochlorite 6.15%) for at least 5 minutes. Lab PPE (gloves, coat, and goggles) will be worn at all times to prevent any harm from fumes/splashed bleach solution. Then, the larvae were properly disposed of and the remaining bleach solution was poured out. This is an ethical euthanization method and is not considered a painful procedure by the Guidelines for Euthanasia of Zebrafish [@NIH_Zebrafish_Euthanasia_2023].

At a higher spatial frequency of 0.12 CPD (@Figure_4), β-sitostenone continued to demonstrate superior therapeutic potential compared to astaxanthin. While astaxanthin treatments (LA+D+E3 and HA+D+E3) yielded no significant recovery in saccadic frequency at any speed, β-sitostenone (LS+D+E3 and HS+D+E3) provided robust rescue at 1 rpm and 2 rpm (p<0.01 and p<0.0001, respectively). However, at 6 rpm, the rescue effect was attenuated; only the low dose β-sitostenone (LS+D+E3) maintained a statistically significant improvement (p<0.05), while the high-dose group (HS+D+E3) did not differ significantly from the DR control. 
## Discussion
This study aimed to evaluate the comparative therapeutic efficacy of astaxanthin and β-sitostenone in a zebrafish model of diabetic retinopathy (DR). While both compounds successfully mitigated systemic cardiovascular symptoms, specifically reducing DR-induced tachycardia, they showed a stark divergence in their ability to preserve neurosensory function. The findings demonstrate that β-sitostenone uniquely rescues the optokinetic response in a dose- and speed-dependent manner, whereas astaxanthin at the tested concentrations failed to provide significant visual recovery. 

A notable finding of this study is the divergence between cardiovascular and neurosensory recovery. Both astaxanthin and β-sitostenone treatments successfully mitigated DR-induced tachycardia, suggesting that both compounds reached systemic circulation and exerted protective effects on the heart rate; however, this systemic alleviation did not translate to functional visual recovery in the astaxanthin-treated groups, which showed no significant improvement in saccadic frequency compared to the DR control. In contrast, β-sitostenone uniquely restored the optokinetic response across multiple spatial frequencies. This suggests that β-sitostenone may possess a specific ability to cross the blood-retinal barrier or target retinal-specific pathways that astaxanthin—at the concentrations tested—could not effectively reach or activate.

Notable inconsistencies were observed in the optokinetic response to varying concentrations of β-sitostenone across different spatial frequencies. In 0.054 CPD trials, the higher concentration (1 µM) consistently yielded a more significant increase in saccadic frequency. Conversely, in the 0.12 CPD trials, representing a finer spatial task, the lower concentration (0.1 µM) appeared to maintain greater significance at higher rotational velocities (6 rpm). This suggests that while β-sitostenone is broadly effective in restoring visual function, its efficacy may be modulated by the complexity of the visual stimulus, with its ability to restore high-frequency tracking potentially being dose- and speed-dependent. The fact that β-sitostenone was able to rescue these high-frequency responses at all further distinguishes the DR-induced deficits from permanent neurodegenerative damage seen in glaucoma models, which typically show a more global and irreversible decline in OKR regardless of stimulus speed.

As the speed of the rotating drum in rpm increases, the optokinetic response should become much more difficult to produce, thus producing a lower saccadic frequency. The same is true of the spatial frequency: as CPD increases, the stimulus actually decreases in width, lowering the optokinetic response. So, at the highest rpm and CPD analyzed, the saccadic frequency was expected to be the lowest, since the larval visual system is pushed to its physiological limit. 

While some studies have suggested that astaxanthin’s antioxidant properties have demonstrated their ability to rescue some of the cellular functions in retinal ganglion cells that are depleted by DR [@dong_protective_2021], this study has found that it may struggle to restore functional tracking at the specific concentrations of 10–100 µg/L when compared to the potency of β-sitostenone; however, it is worth noting that the concentrations listed were based on previous toxicity testing for zebrafish embryos [@dong_protective_2021].

The findings of this study are consistent with literature review regarding the functionality of β-sitostenone in diabetic retinopathy. Previous research has demonstrated that  β-sitostenone has the potential to inhibit angiogenesis by upregulating the expression of glucocorticoid receptors [@ji_stigmast-4-en-3-one_2024]. While current literature has established the molecular framework for β-sitostenone’s anti-angiogenic properties, those findings were limited to in vitro and non-behavioral in vivo models. This study bridges that gap by providing a functional behavioral correlate; the significant recovery in OKR suggests that the molecular stabilization of retinal vasculature directly translates to the presentation of visual acuity and tracking capabilities in a living organism.

Despite the significant functional recovery observed with β-sitostenone, this study has several limitations. The switch to commercially sourced embryos and the loss of transgenic Tg(fli1 : EGFP) line restricted this study from performing detailed angiogenic imaging of the retinal vasculature. Additionally, spatial frequency calculations relied on estimated distances, and the concentrations of astaxanthin may have been insufficient to reach a therapeutic window. Finally, while OKR is a robust measure of visual function, future studies should include intraocular pressure measurements to definitively rule out glaucoma as a source of the saccadic deficits. 

## Conclusion
This study provides preliminary evidence for the use of β-sitostenone as an oral, naturally derived treatment for diabetic retinopathy (DR). Although astaxanthin attenuated the elevated heart rate demonstrated by dextrose-induced DR,  β-sitostenone proved to be more effective in mitigating the ocular and behavioral characteristics of DR. The findings of this study reinforce 130 mM dextrose as a sufficient model of DR and they support the observed molecular capabilities of β-sitostenone with a behavioral model; however, the use of astaxanthin as an anti-DR agent was not supported with the concentrations used for this study.

### Future work
To enhance the statistical reliability and reproducibility of these results, future studies will utilize larger sample sizes of embryos sourced from standardized providers, such as Carolina Biological. Furthermore, refining in-house breeding protocols remains a priority to ensure a consistent supply of healthy larvae for longitudinal studies. To address the performance of astaxanthin, future trials will evaluate a broader range of concentrations. By increasing the dosage while maintaining non-toxic molarity, future studies should aim to determine if a higher therapeutic threshold exists for astaxanthin-mediated visual rescue.

A critical next step involves the use of transgenic fluorescing zebrafish lines, specifically Tg(fli : EGFP), to provide a visual correlate to the behavioral data. This will allow for the precise measurement of retinal blood vessel diameter and macular thickness, enabling future work to determine if the functional recovery in the OKR assay is directly linked to the inhibition of pathological angiogenesis.
Beyond ocular health, the significant reduction in tachycardia observed in this study suggests that β-sitostenone and astaxanthin may have broader applications. Future research should investigate their potential as non-invasive oral treatments for systemic cardiac disorders, such as hypertension and other forms of diabetic-induced cardiovascular stress.
