---
title: "Effects of Epigallocatechin-3-gallate on Β-amyloid (25-35) Aggregation: an Application to Sporadic Inclusion Body Myositis"
abstract:
  Sporadic Inclusion Body Myositis (sIBM) is a late onset rare muscular degenerative disease affecting roughly 7 in 1 million people. It is cellularly characterized by rimmed vacuoles, inflammatory infiltrates, and protein aggregates—namely of beta amyloid. This study investigates epigallocatechin-3-gallate’s (EGCG) potential to hinder or reverse the process of beta amyloid aggregation. With fewer aggregates, sIBM cannot progress as rapidly, meaning that individuals with the disease could maintain fine motor functions for a longer time when consuming an anti-amyloidogenic medication. EGCG, a polyphenol derived from the leaves of *Camellia sinensis*, is known to have antioxidant properties, indicating its potential as a therapeutic drug. In this study, EGCG was first purified from green tea leaf extract, and its composition was confirmed using Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy. The Thioflavine T assay was then used to monitor beta amyloid aggregation under a variety of environmental conditions via fluorescence spectroscopy. When EGCG was present, the protein aggregated at a slower rate over periods of one hour and 24 hours. The trials each yielded critical values less than 0.0001 with a p-value of 0.05. This indicates that EGCG may hinder the aggregation of beta amyloid, suggesting that products of green tea leaves containing EGCG may be therapeutic agents for individuals with sIBM.

## Introduction

### Sporadic Inclusion Body Myositis
Sporadic Inclusion Body Myositis (sIBM) is the most prevalent muscle myopathy affecting older adults [@brady_inclusion_2024]. It affects men more often than women at approximately a 3:1 ratio [@needham_sporadic_2016]. Although it is classified as a myopathy, sIBM bears little resemblance to other myopathies such as Becker Muscular Dystrophy, Congenital Myopathy, and Polymyositis. Rather, it is characterized by rimmed vacuoles, inflammatory infiltrates, and protein aggregates. The aggregates are those of β-amyloid, which accumulate and become toxic to cells after amyloid precursor protein (APP) is cleaved by β-secretase instead of the standard enzyme, α-secretase. 

Early classification of sIBM was flawed, as it was only determined pathologically by the presence of 15-18nm tubulofilaments. Now, it is known that amyloid aggregates are the filament-like structures characteristic of sIBM. This pathological identification, along with clinical presentation of limited dexterity and asymmetric weakness, shapes current diagnosis. However, the disease is widely underdiagnosed—each subsequent study of the prevalence of sIBM indicates larger diagnosis percentages [@brady_inclusion_2024]. 

### Current Treatment Options

Although classified as an inflammatory myopathy, sIBM is unique in that it does not respond to anti-inflammatory medications [@amato_inclusion_2009]. Currently, treatment manifests itself as physical therapy to maintain muscle function and speech therapy to combat swallowing difficulties. Clinical trials for new treatments prove largely unreliable due to their small nature; it is challenging to assemble a group of individuals with sIBM because of its relatively rare nature. 

### Current Research

While there is currently no treatment, the discovery of one is not at the forefront of medical invention. sIBM does not affect the lifespan of the patient, but it severely impacts their quality of life [@clevelandclinic_2024]. Much more research focuses on β-amyloid aggregation in the brain, which is considered a potential cause of Alzheimer’s Disease (AD). Because AD impacts a significantly larger population than sIBM, it is of more consequential concern to many. 
Most *in vitro* β-amyloid research is conducted using amino acid strands 1-42. However, the undecapeptide 25-35 is the shortest sequence capable of forming toxic amyloid aggregates (Naldi, 2012). By isolating this section for research, more aspects of the study can be controlled. Additionally, with a shorter section, the study will be more reproducible. 

### Epigallocatechin-3-Gallate

Epigallocatechin-3-gallate (EGCG), an ester of epigallocatechin and gallic acid, is a polyphenol derived from *Camellia sinesis*—green tea leaves. It has the capability to cross the blood-brain-barrier, making it a potential therapy for a variety of diseases. It has been successful in reshaping amyloid fibrils into non-toxic aggregates in AD transgenic mice [@vassallo_effect_2015]. This indicates that EGCG may have a similar mechanistic effect in the cells of sIBM patients. To test this, β-amyloid can be isolated and its reaction to EGCG can be measured using fluorescence spectroscopy. 

### Principle of the Thioflavine T Assay

The Thioflavine T (ThT) assay is a technique used to quantify the aggregation of amyloid fibrils by measuring relative fluorescence units (RFU) over time. As the aggregates grow, the ThT dye has more binding sites and therefore causes an increase in fluorescence. This strategy for visualization can be used to measure the impact of EGCG on β-amyloid aggregation. The potential of EGCG to deaggregate amyloid fibrils over an extended period of time would have significant implications to treatment of sIBM.

### Research Objectives

This paper will investigate how EGCG impacts the aggregation kinetics of β-amyloid 25-35 *in vitro* using the ThT assay. Both preventative and reactive treatments will be tested to determine optimal concentrations of EGCG in order to make the greatest impact for sIBM patients. 

## Methods

### Epigallocatechin-3-gallate Isolation from *Camellia sinensis* Pills

Green tea extract pills containing a reported value of 45% EGCG were acquired from Horbäach manufacturing. Added ingredients in the pills included rice powder, vegetable magnesium stearate, and silica, while the *Camellia sinensis* natural extract contained a variety of polyphenols, and catechins. For this study, it was desirable to eliminate the added ingredients and isolate the EGCG, along with the other polyphenols. 

To do so, 1.000g of the pill powder was first placed in an 80% ethanol solution to dissolve the polyphenols. The rice powder, vegetable magnesium stearate, and silica were not soluble in the ethanol solution. To remove them, vacuum filtration was utilized with Whatman 3 filter paper. The solution was passed through the filter medium twice to remove most of the particulate matter. 

To further extract non-polyphenolic materials from the *Camellia sinensis* extract pills, the solution was centrifuged  at 6000 rpm for 15 minutes. The pellet was discarded, and the remaining solution was removed with a pipette. 
This solution was then transferred to a rotary evaporator with a warm water bath of 30℃. Over the course of 20 minutes, the pressure was manually decreased from 1000 mbar to 50 mbar in increments of 25 what bar. Complete evaporation of the ethanol solution was achieved after 70 minutes. 

Using the initial and final masses of the powder, the concentration of EGCG was calculated and a stock solution was made with an EGCG concentration of 10000μM. This stock was used for every trial. 

### Epigallocatechin-3-Gallate FTIR Confirmation
Following purification of the *Camellia sinensis* pills, the presence of EGCG was confirmed using Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR). The extract powder resulting from rotary evaporation was combined with potassium bromide at a 1:100 ratio. These substances were homogenized with a mortar and pestle for approximately 120 seconds. Then, a small amount of the resulting powder was compacted under two tons of pressure in a sample holder. The sample was then placed in the spectrometer and scanned. 

### β-Amyloid Monomerization and Aliquot Preparation

β-Amyloid (25-35) with a reported molecular weight of 1060.27g/mol was acquired from GenScript (Cat. No. RP10008-1) and stored at -20℃. The protein was then monomerized through pH change and snap-freezing to prevent premature aggregation [@roberson_preparing_2010]. 1 mg of β-amyloid (25-35) was combined with 4.2477 mL of .05 M NaOH to make a 222 μM β-amyloid (25-35) solution of pH 12. This solution was then thoroughly mixed in the sonicator for 5 minutes at 50 kHz. 21 200 μL aliquots of this solution were prepared in 1mL cryogenic tubes, then individually subjected to snap-freezing through a 7-10 second submersion in liquid nitrogen. Successful freezing was indicated by the discontinuation of the sizzling sound. These monomerized aliquots were then stored in a -80℃ freezer. 

This preparation enabled stable, long-term storage of the protein. When ready for use, these aliquots were individually thawed and never re-frozen, as doing so would alter the protein structure [@roberson_preparing_2010]. 

### Thioflavine T Preparation

Thioflavine T (ThT), a fluorescent benzothiazole dye with a molecular weight of 318.86 g/mol, was acquired from Thermo Scientific (CAS No. 2390-54-7). A stock dilution of 15 μM ThT was prepared by combining 0.0005 g of ThT with 0.1 mL of 1x Phosphate Buffered Saline (PBS). This solution was aliquoted into eppendorf tubes, each containing 1mL. These aliquots were then stored at -20℃ and used as needed; no freeze-thawing occurred. 

### Thioflavine T Assay

A 7.4 pH buffer was made by diluting 10x PBS to a 1x PBS stock solution, which was used for every trial. Four trials were simultaneously tested with 25 μM concentrations of β-amyloid (25-35) protein [@roberson_preparing_2010] and 4 μM concentrations of ThT [@xue_thioflavin_2017]. 1000 μL of each trial was prepared; this resulted in volumes of 112.6 μL β-amyloid (25-35) 222 μM and 266.6 μL of ThT 15 μM. Trial 1 tested with 0 μM EGCG, acting as a control. Trial 2 tested with 5 μM EGCG, trial 3 with 10 μM EGCG, and trial 4 with 15 μM EGCG. These had volumes of 0 μL, .5 μL, 1.0 μL, and 1.5 μL EGCG (10000 μM), respectively. The remaining volume was filled by PBS to reach 1000 μL per trial. Within five minutes of finalizing preparation of each trial, the scan was started. 

Each trial was run concurrently in a black 96-well plate in a BioTek Synergy HTX multiplate reader. A 420/27 wavelength filter was used for excitation, and a 485/20 wavelength filter was used for emission. The fluorescence spectrophotometer was preheated to 37℃ and maintained that temperature for the duration of the reading. A kinetic scan was used, reading the wells one time per minute each for 60 minutes. After the Thioflavine T assay was completed, all materials containing the dye were disposed of properly. Thawed materials were discarded, not refrozen or reused. 

## Results

### Thioflavine T Assay (one hour)

```{figure} images/RFU vs. Time (GS).png
:align: center
:name: Figure_1

This fluorescence versus time graph shows the relative fluorescence units of four different concentrations of EGCG over the course of one hour. 
```

Four concentrations of EGCG were tested in a 7.4 pH solution of PBS, ThT, and β-amyloid (25-35) to elucidate which was most effective in decreasing the aggregation of the protein. The control, an untreated protein solution, showed a 59.96% decrease in fluorescence. The protein solution treated with a 5 μM concentration of EGCG showed a greater decrease in fluorescence of 62.63%. The 10 μM EGCG treated solution showed the most significant decrease of 69.43%, and the 15 μM EGCG treated solution showed a decrease of 62.52%. 

These values were averaged across four repeats of each trial, with fluorescence readings once per minute for an hour each. The error bars correlate to the colors of the respective trials. The untreated solution had a standard error of 21.93, while the others had standard errors of 5.89, 15.07, and 7.37, respectively. 

Because the 10 μM concentration of EGCG showed the greatest percent decrease in relative fluorescence, it was identified as the most effective drug concentration of those tested. Statistical significance of the effectiveness of the drug was indicated by a p<0.05. Comparing the 0 μM EGCG trial with the 10 μM EGCG trial yielded a critical value <0.00001, indicating that EGCG had a notable effect on the structure of the β-amyloid (25-35). Although the 5 μM and 15 μM solutions did not cause as great of a decrease in fluorescence, their effects were also statistically significant.

Using FTIR spectroscopy, the presence of EGCG was confirmed in the powder resulting from the *Camellia sinensis* extract pill purification. A slight shoulder around 3500 cm-1—revealing a carbonyl group—a deep valley around 3360 cm-1—representing hydroxyl groups—and a drop around 1680 cm-1— showing the conjugated ester carbonyl group—prove that the characteristic functional groups of EGCG were present [@moreno-vasquez_characterization_2021]. Slight variation exists between this spectrum and that of literature, but this difference is expected; the purification methods used on the extract pill did not remove other polyphenols and catechins. 

## Discussion
This study suggests that EGCG has significant inhibitory effects on β-amyloid aggregation *in vitro*. These results align with existing literature by suggesting that EGCG can remodel amyloid fibrils into non-toxic, off-pathway oligomers in neurodegenerative disease models [@vassallo_effect_2015].
Although β-amyloid aggregation has received little attention for Sporadic Inclusion Body Myositis, significant research on its role in Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) has been conducted. The structural similarity of  β-amyloid in these two conditions suggests that anti-amyloidogenic research pertaining to AD may also be applicable to sIBM. 
### Short-Term Aggregation Kinetics
Across multiple 60 minute scans, the solutions treated with EGCG showed consistently lower relative fluorescences, as well as greater decreases in fluorescence. Although a 10 μM concentration proved to be the most effective at decreasing β-amyloid aggregation short-term, the three tested concentrations all yielded statistically significant decreases relative to the untreated solution.
The decrease of β-amyloid fluorescence in the presence of EGCG suggests that the catechin reshaped the protein into a smaller—likely  less-toxic—structure, because smaller aggregates decrease the quantity of binding sites between the Thioflavine T and the β-amyloid. However, size-dependent toxicity has not yet been confirmed {cite:p}`https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3028`, meaning that a smaller aggregate may not necessarily be less harmful. However, if this decrease in fluorescence was the result of a structural change within the protein, then there would be a decrease in toxicity. Further experimentation would be required to discern this. 
### Long-Term Aggregation Kinetics
After 24 hours, fluorescence was minimal in all treated solutions, yet still significant in the untreated solutions. This suggests that, at concentrations between 5 μM and 15 μM, EGCG causes not just a slower rate of aggregation of β-amyloid, but also disassembly. This is consistent with the known mechanistic model in which EGCG interacts with amyloidogenic sequences and redirects them into non-β-sheet aggregates [@vassallo_effect_2015]. It further supports that EGCG exhibits both preventative and reactive anti-amyloidogenic activity. 
### Implications
Although EGCG has been studied in the context of Alzheimer’s Disease, its potential use in combatting Sporadic Inclusion Body Myositis has remained largely unexplored, despite the similarities in β-amyloid aggregation between the two diseases. Even when extracted from cost effective, over the counter medication, EGCG significantly altered the aggregation of β-amyloid. This suggests that EGCG-containing drinks, like green tea and matcha, could have therapeutic effects for individuals with amyloidogenic diseases, like sIBM and AD. By slowing the course of these diseases, EGCG could help individuals retain normalcy for much longer. This could lead to both personal benefits and economic benefits, as slower progression of the diseases will keep patients living independently for much longer, enabling them to stay at home. 
### Limitations
Because the Thioflavine T assay was the only method used for examining β-amyloid aggregation,  structural changes cannot be fully discerned from this study—only aggregation changes. The Thioflavine T degraded under light, causing slight decreases during each spectroscopic reading. Additionally, this study was limited to β-amyloid (25-35), meaning that testing was not conducted on the entirety of the amino acid sequence. Although this is the shortest strand of β-amyloid capable of forming toxic fibrils, EGCG may have a different effect when used with the full sequence. Finally, all experimentation was conducted *in vitro*, so the synergistic impacts of EGCG *in vivo* may lead to different conclusions. 
### Future Directions
Future work should incorporate more analysis methods—transmission electron microscopy or atomic force microscopy—to directly visualize the structural changes of β-amyloid when mixed with EGCG. This may reveal the mechanistic effects of EGCG on β-amyloid aggregation, allowing for more conclusions to be drawn about the drug’s effect on the protein. Additionally, *in vivo* experimentation should be conducted to investigate how EGCG acts within the body. This testing could also reveal the effectiveness of oral ingestion of EGCG, as opposed to alternative methods, such as injection. Furthermore, β-amyloid is not the only characteristic of sIBM. Other inflammatory aggregates also become problematic to the cells. Further investigation will be required to determine whether hindering the progression of β-amyloid aggregation will slow the course of the disease, or whether treatment must target the root of the disease—improper cleavage of amyloid precursor protein by β-secretase or α-secretase. Experimentation utilizing the 1-42 amino acid sequence of β-amyloid will also be necessary to discern whether EGCG is similarly effective on the entire length of the protein.
## Conclusion
Overall, this study provides evidence that EGCG both slows and reverses the aggregation of β-amyloid (25-35) *in vitro*. The results suggest statistically significant deaggregation of β-amyloid both short-term and long-term. These findings support further investigation into how EGCG and related polyphenols act as potential therapeutic agents for sIBM and other amyloidogenic diseases.
